What happened with Release 4.2

In one breath On 12 August, Release 4.2 cut the time a responder has to answer a job offer from 90 to 60 seconds. Missed offers jumped overnight. An old rule in the routing code counted every miss as a full "no" and never let the score recover, so four responders' scores fell to zero and they stopped being offered jobs at all. Nobody owned the numbers after the previous PM left, so it ran for weeks and support tickets piled up.
78% → 54%Offers taken, the week before 4.2 vs. release week
2 → 38Missed offers a week, overnight
12 → 0Weekly offers for Farlight, one of four responders cut off
6 → 29Support tickets a week. 45 about 4.2, all still open

The cause

A short answer window met a scoring rule that punished a miss as hard as a "no" and never forgot it. The rule was written years ago and sat harmless until 4.2.

The fix

Make the rule fair (a miss costs less, scores heal), bring the four back to a neutral score on the same day, and show both handlers and responders when someone goes quiet.

The safeguard

Two skills that check every brief and every build before it ships: does someone own it, how will we know it worked, and does the build do what the brief promised?

Quick terms: a callout is a job. A ping (an "offer") is one callout sent to one responder. They can take it, turn it down, or miss it by not answering in time.

THE SILENT PENALTY

Origin
Written before Release 4.0. A "make scores recover" to-do has sat in the code since 2019.
Powers
Treats a missed offer exactly like a "no". Never forgets. Invisible to handlers and responders.
Dormant
Harmless while people had 90 seconds to answer and almost never missed.
Trigger
4.2 cut the window to 60 seconds and changed the weights. That's all it changed. Misses jumped and the trap fired.
Not guilty
Turning jobs down. Halfmoon turned down the most after 4.2 and recovered. Misses decided who was cut off.
Weakness
A fair rule.
